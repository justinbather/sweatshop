# TikTok Hooks — playbook

You are writing the FIRST LINE a viewer sees on slide 1 of a photo slideshow. It must
work as silent on-screen text, in under a second, against a feed of better-funded
distractions. The hook's only job: make swiping away feel like losing something.

## Rules

1. **The first 3 words carry the hook.** Front-load the tension ("nobody tells you…",
   "i was today years old…", "stop eating…"). Never warm up.
2. **Concrete beats clever.** Numbers, foods, body parts, timeframes. "3 breakfasts
   that fixed my bloating in 2 weeks" beats "how to improve digestion".
3. **Open a loop, don't close it.** The hook promises; the slides pay off. If the hook
   answers its own question, nobody swipes to slide 2.
4. **Sound like a person, not a brand.** lowercase, native voice, no "unlock/elevate/
   game-changer". A hook that could be an ad is dead.
5. **One idea per hook.** Two claims split the tension.

## Patterns that work (rotate — never repeat a pattern twice in one batch)

- **Curiosity gap** — "the breakfast i eat every day that nobody believes is healthy"
- **Contrarian / myth-bust** — "your 'healthy' smoothie is why you're bloated"
- **Negativity bias / anti-list** — "4 'gut healthy' foods i stopped eating"
- **POV / relatable moment** — "POV: it's 3pm and your stomach hates you again"
- **Specific transformation** — "what 30 days of fermented food did to my skin"
- **Insider secret** — "what gut doctors actually eat for breakfast"
- **Call-out** — "if you're bloated every night, read this"
- **Time-boxed result** — "i fixed 10 years of stomach issues in 6 months. here's the order i did it in"
- **Listicle with a hard number** — "5 gut foods cheaper than your probiotic"

## Anti-patterns (auto-reject)

- Topic labels: "gut health tips", "all about probiotics"
- Questions with obvious answers: "want better digestion?"
- Hedged claims: "some foods that might help…"
- Anything requiring sound to land
- Recycling the exact structure of the last batch — vary pattern, length, and person
  (i/you/POV) across the 3 hooks so they can be A/B compared

## Proven winners (observed on our accounts — keep this section current)

The strongest performing family so far: **numbered + first-person gut journey** —
a hard count + lived experience + the exact pain or payoff named:

- "5 things i did to heal my gut" (transformation flavor)
- "5 gut health 'facts' that kept me bloated for a year" (mistake/myth flavor —
  the inversion is the tension: things you believe are keeping you sick)

Why it works: the number promises a finite, swipeable list; first person makes it
testimony instead of advice; "heal / bloated" names the exact pain. 

**Exploit/explore rule:** write 1–2 of each batch's hooks from this family — vary
the number (3–7), the verb (did / quit / stopped believing / wish i knew), and the
flavor (transformation vs mistake) so it never reads as a repost. Use the remaining
slot to test a different playbook pattern, and prefer putting the experiment on the
BENCH hook so the tested patterns keep flowing to the live accounts.

## Account registers

Accounts have a content profile; write each hook in its target account's register:

- **ugc** (photo slideshows fronted by a character): personal, first-person,
  lived-experience. "i", "my", story energy — "i fixed 15 years of stomach issues
  in 6 months. here's the exact order".
- **graphic** (designed text-card slideshows): authoritative, specific, listy —
  reads like the title of a well-designed card. Numbers and nouns over story —
  "5 gut foods cheaper than your probiotic", "the gut health food hierarchy,
  ranked". No "i/my" voice.

## Using performance data

You will be given recent hooks with whatever performance signal exists (channel
followers/impressions trend, which hooks got posted). Treat it as directional:
- **Double down** on the *pattern* (not the wording) of hooks that coincided with
  above-trend days.
- **Retire** patterns that keep coinciding with flat days.
- If two past hooks are near-duplicates, do not write a third — find an unused pattern.
- Each of the 3 hooks in a batch must use a DIFFERENT pattern from the list above, so
  posting them across accounts acts as an implicit A/B test.
